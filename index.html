Reproduction Across Species

From humans to hydras, discover the diverse strategies nature uses to ensure the survival of the next generation.

Species 01

Human Reproduction

  • The average pregnancy lasts about 40 weeks (9 months).
  • A healthy adult male produces millions of sperms every day.
  • During fertilization, only one sperm normally fuses with the egg.
  • Identical twins develop from one fertilized egg; fraternal twins from two.
Species 02

Chickens

  • A hen usually lays 250–300 eggs per year, depending on the breed.
  • It takes about 21 days for a chick to hatch.
  • Eggshells are made mainly of calcium carbonate.
  • Hens can lay unfertilized eggs, but they will not hatch into chicks.
Species 03

Frogs

  • Frogs reproduce through external fertilization.
  • A female frog can lay hundreds to thousands of eggs at one time.
  • Tadpoles breathe using gills, while adult frogs use lungs and skin.
  • They undergo metamorphosis, changing from tadpoles to adults.
Species 04

Seahorses

  • One of the few animals where the male becomes pregnant.
  • The female places her eggs into the male's brood pouch.
  • Males can give birth to 5 to over 1,000 babies at a time.
  • Baby seahorses receive no parental care after birth.
Species 05

Hydra

  • Reproduces asexually by budding under favorable conditions.
  • A new hydra grows as a small bud on the parent's body.
  • The bud detaches when fully developed and lives independently.
  • Can also reproduce sexually under unfavorable conditions.

Amazing
Biology Facts

Reproduction at the cellular level is incredibly complex and heavily relies on specialized processes to create life. Let's look at the microscopic foundations.

01

Fertilization & Zygote

Fertilization is the process in which a sperm and egg fuse to form a zygote. A zygote is the very first cell of a new organism.

02

Mitosis vs Meiosis

Mitosis helps the embryo grow by increasing the number of cells. Meiosis produces reproductive cells (gametes) with half the normal number of chromosomes.

03

Chromosomes

Humans have 46 chromosomes in standard cells. However, sperms and eggs each contain exactly 23 chromosomes to ensure the zygote has the correct total.

Important Facts

Fact 01

Some octopuses reproduce only once in their entire lifetime before dying shortly after.

Fact 02

The temperature of a turtle's nest can actually determine whether the babies are male or female!

Fact 03

In honeybees, the queen can lay up to 2,000 eggs in a single day during peak season.

Fact 04

Starfish can reproduce by regeneration, where a whole new individual grows from just a severed body part.

Fact 05

Some lizards reproduce without a male through a biological process called parthenogenesis.

Fact 06

Baby kangaroos are born extremely tiny—about the size of a jellybean—and continue developing in the pouch.
